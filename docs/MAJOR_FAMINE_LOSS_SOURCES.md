# Agricultural production evidence for major historical famines (research inventory)

*Preliminary literature screening, 2026-11-25. Figures below are reported or reconstructed in the cited literature, not new AgRichter results. This is not yet a validated, spatially harmonized quantitative dataset.*

## Scientific framing

The AgRichter potential-loss envelopes are legitimate **complete-disruption end members**. Actual historical production declines, however, arise from environmental, plant-pathogen, political, conflict, and institutional processes. They provide an independent and heterogeneous validation layer. Do **not** equate famine mortality, food access losses, official production changes, and counterfactual crop failure. No single famine necessarily corresponds to a 100% spatial production loss.

## High-priority cases and accessible evidence

### China: Great Leap Forward famine (1959–1961)
- Lin and Yang (2000), *Food Availability, Entitlements and the Chinese Famine of 1959–61*, *Economic Journal*, Table 1 (State Statistical Bureau statistics), reports grain output: **1958 200.0**, **1959 170.0**, **1960 143.5**, **1961 147.5** million metric tons. The 1960 output was **28.25% below 1958**, an observed national-production comparison **not a causal estimate** of the effects of one policy or hazard. Article: https://gwern.net/doc/sociology/2000-lin.pdf
- Same table records **1959 state grain procurement 64.12 Mt**, approximately **37.7%** of reported production, compared with **1958 51.83 Mt** (~25.9%). Production shocks and procurement/access shocks must be tracked as separate variables.
- A county-level study examining output, procurement, mortality, and export mechanisms: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdeveco.2020.102513
- **Data action:** Assemble national annual output/procurement 1954–1966, then provincial time series, source revision and crop composition. Compare only with matching crop sets and spatial footprints. Note differing grain series definitions and revisions across sources.

### Soviet famine explicitly listed in the repository: 1921–1922
- Stephen G. Wheatcroft, *Grain Production and Utilization in Russia and the USSR Before Collectivisation*, doctoral research, gives three competing evaluations of the USSR-less-SCR grain harvest for 1920–1923. Source block 3a totals **34.11 Mt for 1920** and **27.67 Mt for 1921**; the 1920 half-point evaluation is 34.58 Mt (see [ARCHIVAL_RECONSTRUCTION.md](ARCHIVAL_RECONSTRUCTION.md)). No single percent decline is adopted, because the result depends on which evaluation is used. The area is Wheatcroft's USSR-less-SCR, not the full Soviet Union and not the Volga. Source: https://etheses.bham.ac.uk/id/eprint/530/2/Wheatcroft80PhD2.pdf
- University of Warwick's Russian famine collection documents drought, poor harvests, war, requisition, dislocation and trade constraints, and points toward contemporary reports: https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/mrc/collections/digital/russia/famine/
- **Data action:** Extract year-by-region harvest series and harvest methodologies; reconstruct the Volga and other affected regions rather than label all USSR grain production as lost.

### Soviet famine of 1932–1933: distinct candidate, not currently in the 23-event catalog
- Scholarly reappraisals describe disagreement in harvest measurement and counterfactual losses. Secondary discussion reports the USSR 1932 harvest as about 56 million tonnes (Davies and Wheatcroft) and about 50 million tonnes (Tauger). Neither figure was re-read from its primary page in this audit; these refer to a **different famine** than the repo's SovietFamine1921. Source: https://newcoldwar.org/wp-content/uploads/2015/06/Tauger-Natural-Disaster-and-Human-Actions-in-the-Soviet-Famine-of-1931-33.pdf ; further historiographic discussion: https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/09668136.2019.1617464
- **Data action:** Treat as separate event and distinguish Ukraine, Kazakhstan, the Volga, and other affected regions, with crop harvests, procurement, and access data separately.

### Great/Late Victorian famine: 1876–1878 South India (and broader contemporaneous crises)
- *Imperial Gazetteer of India* recounts severe failure of 1876 and 1877 monsoons and crop harvests and documents **83,800 square miles** of Madras as severely famine affected; this is *geographic famine exposure*, not agricultural harvested area or crop loss. Source: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/d/d9/The_Imperial_Gazetteer_of_India_-_Volume_9_%282nd_edition%29.pdf
- A colonial district document for **Tanjore** is reported in this inventory as putting the total harvest well below normal (a figure of 25 percent was noted). The GIPE file did not open when re-retrieved (it returned an HTML bot wall), so the figure is not verified. Tanjore is one district, and more drought-stricken districts may differ widely. Source: https://dspace.gipe.ac.in/xmlui/bitstream/handle/10973/36768/GIPE-017895.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=3
- A secondary copy of the **Coimbatore** district manual is reported as describing very poor crops in the worst 1876–77 season. The primary manual page was not opened, so no fraction is adopted. Secondary access to district manual: https://www.scribd.com/document/841931202/Coimbatore-District-Manual-Nicholson-f-A
- A modern study describes the spatiotemporal drought footprint for the Madras famine but does not by itself estimate crop-tonnage loss: https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1029/2018GL081477
- **Data action:** Identify famine commission district/taluk crop-outturn returns, acreage, and crop mix; the broad label “Great Victorian famine” should not imply all South Asian, Chinese and other concurrent famine regions had identical agricultural shocks.

### Ireland: Great Famine 1845–1852
- Ireland's Central Statistics Office republishes P. M. Austin Bourke's 1959–60 historical analysis of potato crop surveys and notes estimates of **785,308 hectares (1844)**, **829,875 ha (1845)**, **656,014 ha (1846)** planted to potato; **89,000 ha (1847)** followed blight and seed scarcity. These are **areas planted** (not potato tonnage lost), and 1844–45 are retrospective estimates. CSO: https://www.cso.ie/en/media/csoie/releasespublications/documents/agriculture/farmingsincethefamine/General_Details_-_Farming_Since_the_Famine.pdf ; original article reprint: https://www.cso.ie/en/media/csoie/releasespublications/documents/agriculture/farmingsincethefamine/Appendices_-_Farming_Since_the_Famine.pdf
- Bourke cites a contemporary **1846 estimate of potato crop quantity and monetary losses** from Thom's Almanac; early acreage statements use **Irish acres**, not necessarily standard statute acres. Verify units before producing final crop-loss ratios or tonnes.
- Contemporary UK Parliamentary debate includes **1846 estimates for the loss of produce from potatoes and oats**, providing a potentially useful second line of evidence: https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1847-01-19/debates/2fb5b1f9-99e2-4593-a718-9055cb62daee/CommonsChamber
- **Data action:** Reconstruct 1844–1852 potato planted area, area harvested, estimated yield and crop production, then oats and other cereals separately. Potato is NOT within the repository's current “allgrain” crop definition; do **not** plot potato losses in an all-grain production axis without changing and clearly labeling crop scope.

## Harmonization rules

1. Maintain **two axes of evidence**: observed crop production / area loss *versus* food-access, procurement, trade or mortality effects. The latter explains famine severity but does not substitute for production losses.
2. Preserve one row per **country/province/district–crop–year–source**; do not fill missing historic losses with 100% or zero.
3. Record **measurement class** (official output, retrospective reconstruction, contemporary crop-outturn estimate, modeled counterfactual) and original baseline.
4. Convert grain and potato quantities to consistent dry-matter and kcal units only with explicit food-composition, moisture and processing assumptions; do not silently aggregate potato with grain.
5. Use spatially matched modern SPAM potential losses as a **counterfactual comparison**, not as the historical region's realized production.
6. Keep Soviet 1921–22 and 1932–33 separate; and distinguish the 1876–78 Indian famine from the larger class of late Victorian famines.
7. Predefine evidence admissibility before overlaying observations: avoid survivor/selection bias from only quantifiable or severe events.

## Proposed next extraction

Begin with two complete annual national datasets (China 1954–66; Soviet 1918–23), followed by Ireland 1844–52 potato statistics and Indian district-specific 1875–78 crop outturns. Mark each value as observed, reconstructed, or reported estimate, with source location. Extend later to 1932–33 Soviet famine as a separate event. **No numerical AgRichter overlay has yet been calculated from these figures.**
