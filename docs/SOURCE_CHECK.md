# Source check of the unverified crop sentences

*Read 2026-11-26. The registry was not edited. Nothing here is appended to the archival reconstruction or to FAOSTAT. Nothing here is an event loss. Figures 1–4 were not redrawn.*

The machine-readable copy is `ancillary/source_check_v1.csv`. `adopted_as_event_loss` is `no` on every row. A famine, a conflict, a national production level, or a yield gap is not, by itself, a crop loss for a registry case.

## What was opened

**Dust Bowl, national wheat and corn.** Henson (2012) and the cited USDA (2015) edition were not opened. The NASS *Crop Production Historical Track Records* of April 2023 was. In that one national series, all-wheat production is 526,052 thousand bushels in 1934. The 1921–1930 mean of the same column is 826,776.2 thousand bushels, so 1934 is 36.4 percent lower. Corn harvested for grain is 1,146,734 thousand bushels in 1934, against a 1921–1930 mean of 2,197,009.2, which is 47.8 percent lower. Rounded to the nearest percent, those are the compilation’s 36 and 48. The place is the United States. It is not a Great Plains series. *Agricultural Statistics 1937* prints 526,393 thousand bushels for 1934 wheat. That older printing was not used in the ratio.

**1816.** Stommel and Stommel (1983) was not opened, so the compilation’s “almost 100 percent” of New England maize is not confirmed. Monticello quotes Jefferson to Albert Gallatin, 8 September 1816: the corn crop through the Atlantic states will probably be less than one-third of an ordinary one. That is his expectation for a broad region, not a measured New England total.

**Vanuatu, Cyclone Pam, 12–14 March 2015.** The post-disaster needs assessment puts crop-subsector damage at VT 131,596 thousand and crop losses at VT 4,043,278 thousand. Total crop effects are VT 4,174,874 thousand, which the summary figure rounds to VT 4,175 million, 69 percent of agriculture-sector effects. Kava is 58 percent of the crop losses. The figure mixes food gardens and cash crops and is denominated in vatu. It is not a cereal tonnage.

**Sudan, the conflict case.** The March 2026 CFSAM overview estimates 2025 national cereal production, including wheat still to be harvested in March 2026, at about 5.2 million tonnes: 22 percent below the previous year and 19 percent below the previous five-year average. Sorghum is about 4 million tonnes, millet 767,746 tonnes, and the wheat forecast about 433,500 tonnes. The mission attributes the decrease to scarce and expensive inputs, with rainfall offsetting part of the conflict’s constraint. The conflict is not the crop figure, and the figure is national production.

## Compilation sentences that do not survive the page

**Great Famine, 12–44 percent.** Jordan (1996) was not opened. Schuh (2020) reports a different comparison: Titow’s calculation that Winchester yields were 36 percent below average in 1314/15 and 45 percent below average in 1315/16. Titow’s own pages were not opened. The 12–44 percent range stays unverified.

**Ireland, 86 percent.** The CSO page, re-read, prints potato area of 656,014 ha in 1846 and 89,000 ha in 1847, and a pre-famine balance sheet of 15 million tons. It does not say 1847 production fell 86 percent. Bourke (1960) was not retrieved. The area series already transcribed stays the reconstruction.

**Madras, 63–94 percent.** Davis (2001), Table 1.2, prints the percentage of the crop saved in ten Madras districts in 1877: Bellary 6, Kurnool 6, Cuddapah 18, Chingleput 18, Nellore 25, North Arcot 25, Coimbatore 25, Madura 25, Salem 33, Tinnevelly 37. The table’s source line is Temple’s report in the Indian Famine Commission, 1878, page 71. That commission page was not opened. Davis’s prose does not print “63–94 percent lost.” That range is the complement of the 37 and 6 percent saved. Tanjore is not in the table. The same book says that in 1877 half of the Algeria grain harvest was lost, from Oran to Constantine. A separate sentence says wheat and barley exports fell by half. The harvest sentence and the export sentence are not the same quantity.

**Australia, 18–22 percent.** van Dijk and coauthors (2013) say yields during July 2002–June 2009 were 18–22 percent lower than would have been achieved under average conditions. The same paragraph says dryland wheat production volume increased, because a 12 percent yield decline against pre-drought years was offset by a 22 percent increase in area. The 18–22 percent figure is a yield gap against a counterfactual, not a fall in production. The paper’s drought is 2001–2009. The registry window is 2000–2009. BOM (2015) was not opened.

**North Korea, 30 percent.** Goodkind and West (2001) was not opened. Its abstract, as indexed, is a mortality range. Noland, Robinson, and Wang (1999) say the government announced that 330,000 hectares of agricultural land had been destroyed and 1.9 million tons of grain had been lost, and that outside organizations formulated far lower estimates of the damage. Lee (2006) paraphrases 1.9 million tons as about 30 percent of the grain necessary to feed the population, pointing at Goodkind and West, page 221, and a UNDP note. That is a share of a food requirement, not a share of the crop lost. The compilation sentence is not adopted.
